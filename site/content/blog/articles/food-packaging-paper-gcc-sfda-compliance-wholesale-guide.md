## Introduction

The Gulf Cooperation Council market — led by Saudi Arabia and the UAE — is one of the fastest-growing destinations for bakery products, foodservice concepts and private-label dessert brands, and demand for wholesale baking paper, cake cups and foodservice paper packaging is rising with it. But the GCC is not a regulation-free zone where any certificate passes: Saudi Arabia in particular has one of the more active food contact regulatory regimes in the region, and paper packaging shipments that arrive without the right documentation can sit in customs, get sampled by market inspectors, or be rejected during retail supplier onboarding.

For buyers in the region, the compliance question is also a supplier-qualification question: most food contact paper is manufactured in Asia and Europe, and documents that satisfy an EU or US buyer do not automatically map to what a Saudi or Emirati importer needs. This guide explains how GCC food contact requirements work, what the Saudi SFDA expects, which GSO standards apply to paper, and how to build a document package that keeps shipments moving.

## 1. How Food Contact Regulation Works in the GCC

There is no single GCC-wide food contact law. Instead, the region operates through two layers:

- **National authorities.** Saudi Arabia is regulated by the SFDA (Saudi Food and Drug Authority), which issues technical regulations and operates product registration and market surveillance. The UAE operates through authorities including the Ministry of Climate Change and Environment and Dubai Municipality, with its own import and labeling rules. Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman each have food control departments that generally adopt or reference regional standards.
- **GSO standards.** The Standardization Organization for the Gulf (GSO) publishes harmonized technical standards that most member states adopt into national law. For paper and board in food contact, the GSO framework parallels the structure of European regulation: general safety requirements, migration limits, permitted substances and good manufacturing practice expectations.

The practical consequence: a compliant shipment for the GCC typically needs to satisfy both the GSO technical standard and the specific national requirements of the destination country, with Saudi Arabia usually the most demanding.

## 2. Saudi Arabia and the SFDA: What Buyers Should Expect

The SFDA regulates food contact materials, and buyers sourcing paper packaging for the Saudi market should plan for:

- **Technical regulation conformance.** The SFDA adopts GSO-based technical regulations covering food contact paper and board, including expectations on migration behavior, absence of harmful substances and food contact declarations.
- **Import control and sampling.** Food packaging materials entering Saudi Arabia are subject to customs review and can be sampled. Consignments supported by a coherent document package — test reports from accredited laboratories, food contact declarations, certificates of analysis — clear faster and with fewer holds.
- **SFDA registration pathways.** Some importers and license holders require supplier documentation as part of facility licensing and product registration. A bakery chain or food manufacturer onboarding a new packaging supplier will often ask for the full compliance dossier before the first purchase order.
- **Language and format.** Documents in English are broadly workable, but some official processes expect Arabic translations of declarations or certificates. Confirm with your clearance agent which documents need translation before the shipment sails.

## 3. GSO Standards and Test Reports for Paper and Board

The GSO framework for food contact paper draws heavily on international precedents, and test reports for GCC programs generally cover the same territory as European work:

- **Overall migration** limits, typically assessed under standard food simulants and time-temperature conditions appropriate to the paper's end use — baking paper tested under oven-representative conditions, cake cups under their actual fill-and-store conditions.
- **Specific migration and substance screening**, including heavy metals and relevant restricted substances for the colorants, sizing agents and processing aids used in the paper.
- **Contaminant parameters** such as fluorescent whitening agents and formaldehyde, which appear in several regional and national paper specifications.
- **Physical and performance parameters** — grammage, grease resistance where relevant — often checked at import alongside the migration data.

Two practical rules for buyers. First, insist on test reports from laboratories accredited to ISO/IEC 17025; SFDA and UAE authorities weight accredited reports far more than in-house data sheets. Second, match the report to the article: a migration test on unprinted white baking paper does not cover a printed, colored cake cup, which brings inks and colorants into the compliance picture.

## 4. Halal, Labeling and Market Expectations

Beyond food contact regulation, GCC buyers should account for market-specific expectations:

- **Halal relevance.** Paper packaging is not itself halal-certified the way ingredients are, but buyers in the region frequently expect written confirmation that no animal-derived or alcohol-based substances appear in coatings, glues or release agents.
- **Labeling and traceability.** Cases, cartons and retail-ready packaging should carry accurate product identification, batch or production codes and country-of-origin marking consistent with GCC import requirements. Packaging that will itself display retail information — for example, printed cup packaging — must meet national Arabic labeling rules.
- **Climate durability.** Gulf warehousing and transport expose paper goods to high heat and dryness; buyers should confirm that performance parameters are validated for the destination climate, not just the origin factory's conditions.

## 5. Building a GCC-Ready Supplier Document Package

A practical document checklist for a wholesale paper packaging program destined for the Gulf:

1. **Food contact declaration** naming the article, referencing the applicable GSO technical regulation and SFDA requirements, signed and dated by the manufacturer.
2. **ISO/IEC 17025 test reports** for overall and relevant specific migration on the actual article (or an unambiguously equivalent construction), dated within the supplier's stated validity period.
3. **Certificate of analysis** for the shipped batch, linking the compliance file to production traceability.
4. **Coating and additive statement** covering silicone, sizing, colorants and any functional coatings, useful for halal-adjacent questions and for regulator queries.
5. **Manufacturing capability evidence** — a GMP or ISO 22000/FSSC 22000 style certificate — which retail chains in the region commonly require during supplier onboarding.
6. **Import logistics documents** with correct HS classification for the paper articles, prepared consistently with your clearance agent's customs guidance.

Buyers should treat this package as a living file: renew test reports on the schedule the destination authority expects, re-issue declarations when the article or its materials change, and keep the version history organized so the shipment inspection file can be assembled in minutes.

## FAQ

### Do I need a separate compliance file for Saudi Arabia and the UAE?

Usually yes, in effect. Both markets draw on GSO standards, but Saudi SFDA processes, UAE import rules and each authority's document expectations differ in detail. Build one master supplier dossier and assemble market-specific submission packs from it with your clearance agent or regulatory consultant.

### Are EU test reports acceptable for GCC imports?

Often they are a strong foundation — GSO requirements for paper parallel European regulation — but they rarely stand alone. Expect to add a declaration referencing the GSO/SFDA framework and, for some programs, targeted testing against the parameters regional specifications name, such as formaldehyde or fluorescent whitening agents.

### Does halal certification apply to baking paper and cake cups?

Paper packaging itself is not the subject of halal certification schemes the way ingredients are, but buyers in the GCC commonly require written confirmation that no animal-derived or alcohol-based substances appear in coatings, adhesives or release systems. Ask the supplier for an explicit statement rather than assuming.

### How current do test reports need to be?

Validity depends on the article and the authority, but a common working assumption is that migration reports should be no older than two to three years and must reflect the current construction. If the supplier changed paper mill, coating chemistry or ink system since the report, retesting is warranted regardless of the report's age.

## Conclusion

GCC compliance for food packaging paper is a documentation discipline rather than a single certificate. Buyers serving Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf should anchor their programs on the GSO technical framework, satisfy SFDA import expectations with accredited test reports and current declarations, and address market-specific expectations around halal-adjacent statements, labeling and climate suitability. Suppliers who maintain a current, organized dossier make GCC programs routine; suppliers who improvise documents at shipment time create customs risk that no price discount justifies.
