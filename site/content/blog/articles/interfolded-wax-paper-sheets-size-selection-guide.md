## Introduction

Sheet size is the most visible line on an interfolded wax paper quotation, and it is also the specification buyers most often inherit from a previous contract without checking whether it still fits the menu, the dispenser and the service style. The common sizes — 6, 8, 10, 12 and 15 inch — form a ladder rather than a range of equivalents: each step answers a different food load and counter motion, and choosing one step too small or too large shows up as torn wraps, wasted sheets or dispenser misfeeds. This guide works through the size decision in the order a buyer meets it: what the quoted dimension describes, where each size fits, how size interacts with the dispenser box and the pack, and what to confirm in writing before the first carton ships. For the paper side of these programmes, buyers can review our [food-grade die-cut parchment rounds](/products/baking-parchment-paper/) specifications and sample terms.

## 1. What the Quoted Sheet Size Actually Describes

On an interfolded dry wax deli paper specification, the quoted size should describe the cut sheet dimension — the flat sheet as it is slit and folded at the converting stage. That dimension, the base-paper weight and the pack count are three separate variables that must be confirmed independently. Two quotations reading "10 inch" can describe different programmes if one quotes the cut size and the other a folded dimension, so the number, its basis and the tolerance should be stated on the spec sheet. Size also interacts with the converting side: wider sheets are slit from wider base rolls, which affects yield and therefore commercial terms. A supplier who asks for the exact sheet size, weight and pack format before quoting is protecting the delivery timeline rather than delaying it. Both dimensions matter at the counter, so buyers standardising a programme should verify the sheet size against the actual food item and the actual dispenser, not against the previous purchase order. ## 2. Where Each Size Fits: 6, 8, 10, 12 and 15 Inch

The ladder reads as a service map. The 6 inch sheet is the small-format workhorse: basket and tray liners for pastries and cookies, interleaving between stacked baked items, and single-sheet service where the food is picked up rather than wrapped. It is small, fast and lowest in material cost per pull. The 8 inch sheet covers half-wrap service: small sandwiches, burger baskets and counter sheets where one fold completes the job. The 10 inch is the classic deli standard, sized for a full single-sheet wrap around a standard sandwich with room for a clean tuck, which is why it is the default on most interfolded deli programmes. The 12 inch step adds wrap capacity for larger sandwiches, loaded burgers and basket liners on broader trays. The 15 inch sheet serves the oversize end — wraps, burritos and flat-lay tray lining where a single sheet has to cover the whole item. Pick the size from the largest item the sheet must wrap in a single sheet, not from the average one. Resist merging two service styles onto one compromise size, because the waste generated by an oversized sheet is counted in millions of pulls per year.

## 3. Matching Size to Food Load and Service Motion

Size only earns its place when it matches how the sheet is used at the counter. A wrap motion needs diagonal clearance: the sheet has to cover the item plus the fold-over on both ends. A liner motion needs only tray coverage plus a small margin, and a pull-and-toss service needs almost nothing beyond the food footprint. Matching the size to the motion prevents the two most common field failures. Sheets that cannot complete the wrap get doubled up, which doubles the material cost of every order, and sheets so large that half of each one is discarded with the wrapping. The dry wax construction supports both motions: the sheet stays dry-feeling and non-slick, folds cleanly around the food without clinging, and pulls one at a time from an interfolded dispenser. The size still has to be proven against the real menu item during sampling. Where a programme also lines baking trays and moulds behind the counter, the same size logic applies to the tray-lining side. ## 4. Dispenser Box Fit, Packing and Spec Discipline

The sheet size feeds directly into the pack. Interfolded sheets are packed in fixed counts per box — the standard 500-sheet dispenser box on this programme — and the box dimensions change with the sheet size. Pack height, fold geometry and dispenser compatibility should be confirmed together with the size, not assumed from a previous order. Larger sheets fill a box faster in caliper terms, which shifts the pack height and the feed behaviour in the dispenser, so the sample trial should run the actual size in the actual box at the actual counter. Storage follows the same discipline: waxed paper is still paper, and cartons should be stored sealed and rotated like any food-contact consumable. On the commercial side, MOQ and terms depend on the sheet size, the base weight, the pack format and the confirmed specification. Write all four into the purchase order, retain a sealed sample of the approved size, and require food-contact documentation for the shipped grade against the destination-market benchmarks — FDA 21 CFR Part 176, LFGB/BfR Recommendation XXXVI or EU Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 — with declarations per shipment. ## FAQ

### What size interfolded wax paper sheet is the standard for deli sandwiches?

The 10 inch sheet is the most commonly specified single-sheet wrap for standard deli sandwiches, but the right size depends on the largest item on the menu and the wrap motion at the counter. Confirm with a physical sample rather than a default.

### Can one sheet size cover both sandwich wrap and basket liner service?

Sometimes, but usually at a cost. A size chosen for wrapping is often oversized for basket lining, and the waste is multiplied across high-volume pulls. Operations with distinct service styles usually keep them as separate size line items.

### Does the sheet size affect the dispenser box?

Box dimensions, pack height and feed behaviour all change with the sheet size, so the size, the 500-sheet pack format and the dispenser should be trialled together during sampling.

### What should be confirmed in writing before ordering?

Four things: the quoted sheet dimension and its basis, the base-paper weight, the pack count and box format, and the food-contact documentation for the exact construction and destination market. MOQ and terms depend on size, pack format and the confirmed specification.

## Conclusion

Choosing among 6, 8, 10, 12 and 15 inch interfolded wax paper sheets is a service decision. Size from the largest item the sheet must wrap, match the size to the counter motion, and confirm the size, base weight, pack format and documentation together before the first carton ships. Buyers who lock those four variables into the specification, and prove them with physical samples in the real dispenser with the real menu item, get a deli programme that holds across stores and reorders.
