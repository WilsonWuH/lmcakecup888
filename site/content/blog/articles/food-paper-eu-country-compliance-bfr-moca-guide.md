## Introduction

The European Union regulates food contact materials through a horizontal framework — Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 — but it has never completed a harmonized specific measure for paper and board. In practice, the actual quality bar for baking paper, cake cups and food packaging paper is set at national level, and it is not the same in every country you sell into. A paper that satisfies a generic "EU compliant" declaration may still fail a German retailer's BfR check, an Italian authority's MOCA inspection or a French distributor's documentation review. For wholesale buyers supplying multiple EU markets, this gap between framework and national rules is the most common source of compliance surprises.

This guide explains the three national systems buyers encounter most — Germany's BfR recommendations, Italy's MOCA regime and France's national arrangements — how they differ, how mutual recognition works in theory and practice, and how to structure supplier documentation so one paper grade can serve several markets without re-negotiating compliance every time.

## Why "EU Compliant" Is Not a Complete Answer

Regulation 1935/2004 sets the principles: materials must not transfer their constituents to food in quantities that endanger health or change composition or organoleptic properties. Regulation (EU) 2023/2006 requires good manufacturing practice. But the EU has no specific harmonized measure for paper and board, so member states apply their own rules, and the strictest national rule effectively becomes the de facto export standard for suppliers serving the whole region.

The buyer consequence is concrete: your declaration of compliance should name not only 1935/2004 but the specific national measures that apply to your destination markets. A declaration citing only the framework regulation is, for paper, a statement of principle rather than evidence.

## Germany: BfR Recommendation XXXVI

Germany's Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) publishes "Recommendations on Food Contact Materials," and paper and board fall under Recommendation XXXVI ("Paper and Board for Food Contact"). Although formally recommendations, they function as the de facto legal benchmark in Germany: enforcement authorities and retail QA teams treat BfR XXXVI compliance as the expected standard, and deviations are difficult to defend commercially.

For buyers, the practical points are:

- **Composition limits.** BfR XXXVI and its annexes restrict the substances used in paper production and, through related recommendations, set expectations for coatings such as silicone.
- **Testable parameters.** Buyers commonly verify formaldehyde, heavy metals and extraction behavior through recognized test protocols; for coated baking papers, the coating must also satisfy the applicable BfR recommendation.
- **Documentation style.** German customers typically expect the declaration of compliance to cite BfR XXXVI explicitly, plus test reports against its parameters.

If your distribution includes German retail or industrial bakery customers, BfR XXXVI is not optional — it is the entry ticket.

## Italy: MOCA and the National Decree Framework

Italy operates a detailed MOCA regime (Materiali e Oggetti a Contatto con Alimenti). Food contact materials must comply with the national framework built on ministerial decrees that implement and extend the EU framework, notably the decree of 21 March 1973, which sets positive lists and limits for the substances permitted in food contact materials — including paper.

What this means in practice for buyers:

- **Positive-list logic.** The 1973 decree defines which substances may be present and with which limits. A paper grade formulated only against German or US practice may use an ingredient not on the Italian list, which is a real compliance failure in Italy even if it is acceptable elsewhere.
- **Documentation culture.** Italian authorities and large retail groups expect Italian-language declarations, test reports referencing the national decrees and clear supply chain traceability; distributors routinely ask suppliers to confirm MOCA compliance specifically.
- **Purity criteria for auxiliaries.** Processing aids and contaminants carry specific limits, so test reports should cover paper-relevant parameters such as heavy metals and migration behavior.

Buyers serving Italy should request a supplier statement that names the applicable ministerial decrees and confirms formulation conformity to the national positive lists.

## France: National Arrangements and the AKS Poster

France applies its own arrangement of decrees and orders for food contact materials, historically structured around the arrêté framework, with paper and board addressed through national provisions and information notes. French practice is known for two buyer-relevant features:

- **The "AKS" information poster.** France requires food business operators to display a poster summarizing food contact material regulations, including paper, which drives distributors to request explicit compliance references from suppliers.
- **Specific expectations on documentation.** French retail chains commonly ask for declarations citing the French provisions in addition to 1935/2004, plus test evidence from accredited laboratories.

For a supplier serving France, the declaration should reference the French arrangements applicable to paper and board, and the buyer should verify that test reports come from laboratories recognized in the French market.

## Mutual Recognition: Principle and Practice

The EU mutual recognition principle (Regulation (EU) 2019/515) holds that a product lawfully sold in one member state cannot be rejected in another solely because national rules differ, unless the importer can demonstrate a legitimate public-health concern. In theory, a paper compliant with BfR XXXVI alone could circulate across the EU; in practice, retailer requirements, language demands and enforcement habits make relying on mutual recognition slow and risky. The efficient path for multi-market wholesale suppliers is to formulate against the strictest applicable set — typically BfR XXXVI plus the Italian decrees — and document against all destination markets at once.

## Structuring Supplier Documentation for Multi-Market Sales

A documentation set that serves several EU countries should include:

1. A declaration of compliance citing Regulation 1935/2004, Regulation 2023/2006, and the named national measures: BfR Recommendation XXXVI for Germany, the Italian ministerial decrees for Italy, and the French arrangements for France.
2. Test reports from accredited laboratories against the defined parameters, refreshed on a set cycle and tied to the formulation that ships.
3. GMP evidence, since 2023/2006 compliance is checked through the supplier's manufacturing controls.
4. Formulation change notification: any change in paper, coating or process should trigger a documentation review before the next lot ships.
5. Retained samples per lot, so a market inspection can be answered with the exact article that was sold.

## FAQ

### Is BfR XXXVI legally binding outside Germany?

Not directly — BfR recommendations are national guidance, not EU law. But for sales into Germany they function as the commercial and enforcement benchmark: German authorities and retailers treat BfR XXXVI conformity as the expected evidence of safety for paper and board. Buyers exporting to Germany should require it in the specification.

### What is MOCA compliance for food packaging paper in Italy?

MOCA (Materiali e Oggetti a Contatto con Alimenti) is Italy's national regime for food contact materials. For paper, compliance centers on the ministerial decree of 21 March 1973 with its positive lists and limits, supported by Italian-language declarations, test reports and supply chain traceability. A paper sold in Italy should be formulated against those national lists, not only against EU framework principles.

### Can one declaration of compliance cover all EU countries?

Yes, if it is built for it. A multi-market declaration should cite the EU framework regulations plus the specific national measures of each destination market — BfR XXXVI for Germany, the Italian decrees for Italy, the French arrangements for France — and be supported by test reports against those parameters. A declaration that cites only Regulation 1935/2004 covers principles, not the national specifics, and will be challenged in stricter markets.

For food contact paper, Europe's single market is enforced one country at a time: BfR XXXVI sets the expectation in Germany, the MOCA decrees define the lists and limits in Italy, and France applies its own arrangements with distinctive documentation demands.
