## Introduction

When commercial buyers look to source custom printed greaseproof paper wholesale, the primary objective is to align brand aesthetics with stringent food-contact safety and functional performance. Securing a reliable printed food wrapping paper supplier involves far more than simply submitting a logo and waiting for delivery. It requires a comprehensive approach to specifying sheet or roll formats, managing artwork files and ink coverage, navigating complex food-contact regulatory frameworks, conducting rigorous sample testing, and establishing clear inspection criteria.

This guide shows buyers how to define the application, prepare artwork, evaluate set-off and odor, approve finished samples, review food-contact evidence and inspect printed production.

## 1. Defining Application and Format Specifications

The foundation of a successful wholesale order lies in precisely defining how the paper will be used and the exact physical format required. Greaseproof paper is highly versatile, but its physical specifications must closely match the operational realities of the end-user.

### Analyzing the Food Application

Before specifying dimensions or selecting a printed food wrapping paper supplier, buyers must document the exact food application. The temperature of the food, its moisture content, and its fat or oil profile dictate the required grease resistance (often measured by the KIT test). Wrapping a hot, heavily greasy burger requires a different paper specification than lining a tray for dry pastries. Defining the exact food type ensures that the base greaseproof paper will provide adequate holdout and prevent the transmission of oils through the paper to the consumer’s hands.

### Sheet, Roll, and Dimensional Formats

Buyers must decide between precut branded greaseproof paper sheets and continuous rolls, depending on the speed and mechanics of the food preparation line.

- **Sheets:** State length, width, grain direction if relevant, dimensional tolerance and dispensing method.
- **Rolls:** State web width, core, outer diameter or length, winding direction, splice rules and unwind requirements.

### Base Paper Specifications

In addition to size, the basis weight (measured in Grams per Square Meter, or GSM) must be specified. State the offered basis weight and tolerance, then test flexibility, strength, grease holdout and feed behavior. Also approve the base shade because white and natural papers can produce different printed color results.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Precut sheet | Hand wrapping, basket lining or interleaving | Dimensions, grain, cutting and dispensing |

| Continuous roll | Dispenser or converting equipment | Core, width, winding, splices and tension |

## 2. Artwork, Dielines, and Revision Processes

Printed results depend on the paper, print process and artwork. Provide vector files, repeat layout, color references and an approved physical target. The [custom cupcake liner artwork checklist](/resources/custom-cupcake-liner-artwork-checklist/) covers file-control principles that also apply here.

### Preparing Artwork, Dielines, and Color Targets

Artwork must be submitted in vector formats (such as Adobe Illustrator or EPS) rather than rasterized images. Because custom printed greaseproof paper wholesale orders are often printed using flexography, continuous step-and-repeat patterns (scatter prints) are highly popular and cost-effective. If the design requires precise registration to a specific sheet size (registered print), accurate dielines must be established.

Color targeting on greaseproof paper requires careful management. A screen or coated color book is only a reference. Agree the color target and tolerance on the actual substrate, then approve a physical press sample because base shade and ink absorption affect appearance.

### Print Side and Ink Coverage Limits

Specify which side is printed and which side contacts food. Review set-off and migration controls for the actual stacked or rolled construction; do not assume that outside printing removes every transfer route.

When designing the artwork, buyers must be acutely aware of ink coverage. Heavy, 100% solid ink coverage over the entire sheet is strongly discouraged. High ink coverage significantly increases the drying time, raises the risk of ink set-off (transfer from the printed side to the food-contact side while stacked or rolled), and dramatically increases the likelihood of residual odors. Best practices involve using lighter ink coverage, negative space, and repeating logos rather than solid background colors.

### The Revision and Proofing Cycle

The supplier will generate a digital proof demonstrating the layout, repeat length, and dieline positioning. Buyers must review this proof meticulously for spelling, logo orientation, and scale. It is crucial to understand that digital proof approval or general artwork approval is strictly an approval of the visual layout; **artwork approval is not regulatory approval**. It does not signify that the design is inherently safe for food contact, nor does it override the physical limitations of the printing process.

## 3. Food-Contact Safety and Regulatory Document Review

Procuring custom printed greaseproof paper wholesale requires navigating strict regulatory frameworks. Because printing introduces chemical mixtures (inks, varnishes, solvents) to the packaging material, rigorous document review and compliance checks are mandatory.

### EU Regulation 1935/2004 and GMP 2023/2006

For buyers operating in or distributing to the European market, compliance with the general food-contact framework is non-negotiable.

- **EU Regulation 1935/2004:** This overarching regulation stipulates that materials and articles intended to come into contact with food must not transfer their constituents to food in quantities that could endanger human health, bring about an unacceptable change in the composition of the food, or cause a deterioration in its organoleptic characteristics (taste and odor).
- **Regulation (EC) No 2023/2006 (Good Manufacturing Practice - GMP):** This regulation is particularly critical for printed food packaging. It details specific controls relevant to printing inks applied to the non-food-contact side. A major focus of GMP is preventing "set-off"—the invisible transfer of ink components from the printed exterior of one sheet (or roll layer) to the unprinted, food-contact interior of the adjacent sheet when tightly packed.

### FDA Regulatory Status Considerations

For the United States market, the regulatory landscape is governed by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It is essential to note that there is no singular "FDA approved" stamp for a finished piece of printed paper. Instead, the FDA regulatory status depends entirely on the specific components used (the base paper fibers, the sizing agents, the greaseproofing chemicals, and the ink formulations) and the intended use conditions (temperature, food type, duration of contact). Buyers must ensure their supplier can provide documentation demonstrating that all components comply with relevant Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Title 21 parts for their specific intended use.

### Understanding Set-Off, Ink Transfer, and Odor

Buyers often encounter marketing claims of "food-safe inks," but such blanket claims are technically inaccurate and legally insufficient. Regulatory compliance is about the finished article and the prevention of migration. If an ink component transfers (migrates) into the food beyond established specific migration limits (SMLs), the product is non-compliant, regardless of the ink's marketing label.

Similarly, low odor is a critical requirement for food packaging. Odor must be evaluated on the finished product after the actual print and curing process. Review product-specific evidence and the supplier's controls rather than relying on a blanket low-odor claim. ## 4. Sampling, Testing, and Real-World Evaluation

Before committing to full commercial production of custom printed greaseproof paper wholesale, buyers must insist on physical sampling and conduct rigorous real-world testing. Theoretical specifications on paper mean nothing if the product fails in the kitchen or leaves a chemical taste on the food.

### Printed Sample Evaluation (Rub Testing and Odor)

Once physical samples of the custom deli paper are received, they must be evaluated for print stability and organoleptic neutrality.

- **Rub Testing:** The printed surface should be subjected to rub testing to ensure the ink is properly cured and anchored to the paper. If the ink easily smudges, flakes off, or transfers onto hands during normal handling, it poses a risk of contaminating the food environment and indicates poor manufacturing control.
- **Odor check:** Compare finished printed samples with an approved control using a written sensory procedure suited to the buyer's food and pack. Record conditioning, assessors and acceptance criteria.

### Actual Food and Application Testing

The most critical phase of sample evaluation is testing the printed greaseproof paper with the exact food items it is designed to hold. This is a mandatory step.

- **Grease Holdout Validation:** Wrap the actual hot, oily, or greasy food in the custom printed sheets. Observe the paper over the intended duration of use (e.g., the time a burger sits in a warming chute or the time a sandwich spends in delivery transit). Assess whether the grease penetrates the paper, compromising the structural integrity or the consumer experience.
- **Thermal Performance:** If the paper will be subjected to heat lamps, microwaves, or hot holding cabinets, test the paper under these exact conditions to ensure the ink does not degrade, emit odors, or transfer under elevated temperatures.
- **Moisture Management:** Evaluate how the paper handles steam and condensation. Some dense greaseproof papers can trap moisture, making bread soggy, while others breathe slightly. The application test ensures the correct balance is achieved.

## 5. Retail and Foodservice Packing Requirements

The manner in which custom printed greaseproof paper wholesale orders are packed is critical for supply chain efficiency, inventory management, and end-user convenience. Buyers must clearly define their packing requirements based on whether the product is destined for back-of-house foodservice or retail shelves.

### Foodservice Bulk Packaging

For foodservice, choose inner protection, count and dispensing method from kitchen handling and replenishment. Confirm that the pack keeps sheets clean, flat and identifiable through distribution and use.

### Private Label Retail Formats

When sourcing private label greaseproof paper intended for direct-to-consumer retail sale (e.g., home baking supplies), the packing requirements are vastly different. For a broader look at wholesale sheet logistics, consult our [greaseproof paper sheets wholesale buyer guide](/resources/greaseproof-paper-sheets-wholesale-buyer-guide/).

- **Retail Cartons:** Sheets or small consumer rolls must be packed into branded, retail-ready folding cartons. These cartons require their own artwork approval, barcode verification, and structural design.
- **Master Cartons and Palletization:** Both foodservice and retail products require robust master cartons for shipping. Buyers must specify carton labeling requirements (SKU, batch numbers, PO numbers) and palletization standards (pallet dimensions, maximum height, stretch wrapping) to ensure seamless integration into their distribution centers.

## 6. Quality Inspection and Change Control

Maintaining consistent quality across multiple production runs requires establishing clear inspection protocols and change management procedures with the printed food wrapping paper supplier.

### Pre-Shipment Inspection Protocols

Buyers should implement pre-shipment inspections to verify that the finished goods meet the agreed-upon specifications before leaving the manufacturing facility. While buyers should not expect a fixed AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) to apply universally without prior rigorous statistical agreement, standard inspections should include:

- **Visual Inspection:** Checking for printing defects, misregistration, smudging, or unacceptable background spotting.
- **Dimensional Checks:** Measuring sheets or rolls to ensure they fall within the agreed cutting tolerances.
- **Packaging Verification:** Ensuring all inner wrapping, master cartons, and pallet labels match the purchase order instructions accurately.
- **Functional Quick-Checks:** Performing basic rub tests and odor assessments on the production line samples.

### Managing Artwork or Material Changes

Change control is a vital aspect of ongoing supplier management. If the buyer decides to update the artwork (even a minor logo shift), or if the supplier wishes to change the base paper supplier or the ink formulation, a formal change control process must be triggered.
Any change in raw materials necessitates a completely new review of food-contact regulatory documents. A new ink formulation may have different set-off characteristics or require different curing times. Therefore, any modification to the product or process must require new product-specific evidence and potentially new actual food/application sample tests before commercial production resumes.

## FAQ
### How does custom printed greaseproof paper wholesale differ from standard wrapping paper?

Greaseproof paper is selected for oil and fat holdout, while printed food-contact packaging also requires controls for ink transfer, set-off and odor. The exact finished SKU and intended use must be supported; the product name alone proves neither compliance nor performance.

### Can a printed food wrapping paper supplier guarantee an exact Pantone color match?

A supplier can work to an agreed color target, but the acceptance tolerance should be set on the actual paper and approved physical sample. Substrate shade, absorption and process variation affect the result.

### Why is 100% solid ink coverage discouraged for custom deli paper?

Heavy ink coverage significantly increases the risk of ink set-off (transfer to the food-contact side), dramatically slows drying times, and greatly increases the likelihood of strong residual solvent odors that can taint the food. Repeating logos or scattered patterns are highly recommended instead.

### Does artwork approval serve as proof of food-contact safety compliance?

Absolutely not. Artwork approval simply confirms the visual layout, spelling, and positioning of the design. It is completely separate from regulatory compliance. Buyers must independently verify that the base paper and ink formulations meet relevant EU or FDA food-contact regulations for their specific application.

### What is the process for creating private label greaseproof paper for retail?

Define the printed paper and retail pack as linked specifications. Approve both artworks, barcode, count, language, materials and packed-product sample before production.

### How do I know if branded greaseproof paper sheets will hold up to my food product?

The only definitive way to know is through actual food and application testing. You must request finished printed samples from the supplier and test them by wrapping your specific hot, greasy, or moist food items under exact operational conditions to verify holdout, print stability, and odor neutrality.

### What does "set-off" mean in roll or sheet printing?

Set-off occurs when the ink printed on the outside (non-food-contact side) of a sheet transfers onto the unprinted inside (food-contact side) of the sheet stacked directly above it, or the layer rolled tightly against it. Managing set-off is a critical requirement of GMP regulations to prevent invisible chemical migration into food.

### Are there blanket guarantees for "low odor" or "food-safe ink" available?

Buyers should be highly skeptical of marketing claims offering fake low-odor guarantees or blanket "food-safe ink" certifications. Regulatory status depends on all components and the intended use. Proper curing and testing of the finished printed sample are required to verify odor performance and migration safety.
