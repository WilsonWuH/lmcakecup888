## Introduction

Bleaching is one of the least visible decisions in a baking paper or cake cup program — and one of the most common compliance questions retail and foodservice customers now ask. When a supermarket category manager or a QSR procurement team requests "chlorine-free" packaging, they are asking how the pulp behind your paper was bleached: with elemental chlorine gas, chlorine dioxide, oxygen-based chemicals, or none at all. The difference shows up in effluent chemistry, in certification scope and increasingly in whether your product passes the gate at large chains. Every document in our compliance library is tied to a specific construction; see how the [pre-cut parchment paper sheets and rounds](/products/baking-parchment-paper/) programme aligns test reports with the exact grade supplied.

For buyers, the practical challenge is not chemistry — it is verification and specification. Suppliers describe their paper as "environmentally friendly bleaching" with little precision, while buyers need a term they can write into a specification, a document that proves it, and an understanding of how the choice affects price, shade and supply. This guide explains the ECF/TCF landscape for wholesale baking paper, cake cups and food packaging paper, and how to build it into a procurement program. ## 1. The Bleaching Spectrum: What ECF, TCF and PCF Actually Mean

Bleaching terms describe the chemicals used to remove residual lignin and reach target brightness: Documentation scope is confirmed per SKU and destination market, as outlined on our [custom printed baking parchment paper](/products/baking-parchment-paper/) page.

- **ECF (Elemental Chlorine Free):** no elemental chlorine gas is used; bleaching relies mainly on chlorine dioxide, sometimes combined with oxygen, peroxide or ozone stages. ECF is the dominant process for bleached kraft pulp worldwide and delivers high brightness at good cost.
- **TCF (Totally Chlorine Free):** no chlorine chemistry at any stage — only oxygen, ozone, peroxide and enzyme-based sequences. TCF pulp typically carries a modest brightness and cost premium and is less widely available.
- **PCF (Processed Chlorine Free):** applies to recycled-content paper, meaning the recycled fiber was reprocessed without chlorine. Recycled pulp cannot be certified TCF because its original bleaching history is unknown; PCF is the correct term for that case.
- **Unbleached:** no bleaching step at all — the familiar brown kraft. Many foodservice programs choose unbleached baking paper and cups precisely to avoid the discussion entirely.

Two clarifications matter for buyers. First, "chlorine free" in marketing copy is not a defined term; the specification should name ECF, TCF or PCF explicitly. Second, ECF is not literally chlorine-free — chlorine dioxide is still a chlorine compound — but it eliminates the dioxin-forming chemistry of older processes, and most retail packaging requirements in practice accept ECF. TCF is requested mainly by premium organic and natural-food brands. The [factory and certificate library](/factory-certificates/) lists the third-party audits and test reports behind these claims.

## 2. Why It Matters: Compliance, Retail Requirements and Brand Positioning

The drivers behind a chlorine-free requirement are mostly downstream of your own factory:

- **Retail and foodservice buyer requirements.** Large grocery chains and foodservice groups increasingly include bleaching criteria in packaging vendor standards, often bundled with FSC or PEFC sourcing. Failing to answer with a certificate can cost you the listing regardless of product performance.
- **Effluent and environmental scrutiny.** Bleaching chemistry is a headline topic in paper industry environmental assessments. Buyers with ESG reporting obligations want documentation that their packaging inputs are part of the improvement story.
- **Food contact context.** Bleaching method is relevant — though not identical — to food contact safety. What matters for compliance is the full food contact dossier, with bleaching method as a supporting data point.
- **Brand positioning.** Natural and organic bakery brands often want the whole story aligned: unbleached or TCF paper, certified fiber, plastic-free coatings. For these customers the bleaching claim is part of the product's price justification.

## 3. Verifying the Claim: Certificates and Documents That Hold Up

"ECF" on a brochure is not evidence. The documents that actually verify a bleaching claim:

1. **Chain of custody certificates (FSC or PEFC)** from the paper mill — these certify fiber origin, with the certificate scope statement listing the mills and sites covered. Ask for the certificate number and verify it in the public database.
2. **Mill declarations.** A written statement from the pulp or paper mill declaring the bleaching sequence used (e.g., "ECF, D0-Eop-D1 sequence") for the specific grade. For TCF claims, this is the core document.
3. **Third-party eco-labels.** Nordic Swan, EU Ecolabel and Blue Angel include bleaching chemistry criteria in their paper requirements — holding the label implicitly verifies the bleaching limit.
4. **The food contact dossier.** The grade's compliance documents (FDA 21 CFR 176, EU 1935/2004) remain the baseline for food safety.
5. **Change control linkage.** The bleaching claim should be tied to the change control clause of your supply agreement: if the mill changes pulp source or bleaching chemistry, the buyer is notified. Without this, an ECF claim can silently lapse on a reformulated grade.
A practical verification step: request the declaration for the exact grade and grammage you buy, not a general mill statement — bleaching sequences can differ across a mill's grade range.

## 4. Specifying Bleaching in Your Procurement Documents

Translate the claim into specification language:

- State the requirement: "Pulp bleaching: ECF or better; TCF preferred for [product line]. No bleaching with elemental chlorine or hypochlorite."
- Name the evidence: mill declaration per grade, FSC/PEFC certificate number, eco-label certificate where applicable — and require copies at program start and renewal.
- Set the shade consequence: bleaching method correlates with achievable brightness. If you specify TCF, expect a shade and brightness discussion; agree the target shade with tolerance in the same document so the environmental requirement and visual standard are consistent.
- Cover recycled grades with PCF language, and unbleached grades with an explicit "unbleached, no optical brightening agents" clause if you are selling into natural-food channels — some unbleached papers still carry OBA.

## 5. Price, Supply and Program Design

The commercial realities buyers should plan around:

- **ECF is the default.** For conventional grades, ECF adds no meaningful cost — most bleached kraft available today is already ECF. The work is documentation, not substitution.
- **TCF carries a premium and thinner supply.** TCF pulp production is concentrated among fewer mills, mostly in Europe. Expect a modest price premium and longer qualification when switching.
- **Unbleached avoids the issue but changes the brief.** Moving a cake cup line from white to unbleached is a visual identity decision as much as an environmental one — coordinate with the brand owner first.
- **Bundle the claims.** Buyers get the best value when FSC sourcing, chlorine-free bleaching and plastic-free or PFAS-free coating statements are collected as one documentation package per grade, maintained like any other compliance document.

## FAQ

### Is ECF paper acceptable if my customer asks for "chlorine-free"?

In most retail packaging standards, yes — ECF is the accepted interpretation of chlorine-free for virgin fiber. Ask the customer for their vendor standard document; if it names TCF explicitly, only TCF grades will satisfy it, so qualify supply before committing.

### Does bleaching method affect food contact compliance?

Compliance is governed by the full food contact dossier — migration behavior and applicable regulations — not by the bleaching label alone. Both ECF and TCF grades can fully comply; use the bleaching claim as a sustainability and market-access attribute, and keep the food contact documents as the safety baseline.

### How do I verify a supplier's ECF claim before placing a large order?

Request the mill declaration naming the grade and bleaching sequence, the FSC or PEFC certificate number (verify it in the public database), and any eco-label certificate. For multi-year programs, add a change control clause requiring notification if the mill or bleaching chemistry changes.

### What is PCF and when is it the right term?

PCF (Processed Chlorine Free) applies to recycled-content paper: the recycled fiber was reprocessed without chlorine chemistry. Because the original fiber's bleaching history is unknown, recycled grades cannot be called TCF — PCF is the technically correct claim to write into specifications for recycled baking paper and liners.
