## Introduction

Most baking paper and cake cups sold into North America, Europe and the Middle East are manufactured in China — which means buyers sit between two regulatory worlds. The destination market sets the bar that legally matters on arrival: FDA rules in the United States, Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 in the EU. But the factory that makes the product is regulated domestically by China's national food contact standards, the GB 4806 series, and a supplier's depth of GB 4806 compliance says a great deal about its quality systems, testing discipline and ability to hold export-market compliance over time. This guide explains what the GB 4806 framework covers, which parts apply to paper and paperboard, what documentation a credible manufacturer should produce, how to read a test report critically, and how GB 4806 evidence fits alongside the FDA and EU documents you actually need for import.

## 1. What the GB 4806 Framework Covers

China's food contact material system is built on a pyramid of national standards:

- **GB 4806.1 — general safety requirements** for all food contact materials and articles: the principles — safety in intended use, no unacceptable transfer to food, good manufacturing practice.
- **GB 4806.8 — paper and paperboard.** This is the part that governs baking paper, cake cups, wrapping papers and other paper food contact materials: sensory requirements, heavy metal limits, fluorescent whitening agents, formaldehyde and migration-related parameters.
- **GB 9685 — additives.** The positive list governing which additives may be used in food contact materials, including those in paper coatings and treatments, with migration limits.
- **Testing methods (GB 31604 series)** — the standard methods labs use to generate the migration and content results behind a compliant report.

The series has been progressively revised, and the paper standard has been updated in recent years to tighten migration requirements. The practical buyer takeaway: always check which version of each standard a test report cites. The [EU vs US compliance comparison](https://www.lmcakecup.com/resources/eu-us-baking-paper-compliance-comparison/) explains the export-market side in detail.

## 2. What GB 4806.8 Means for Baking Paper and Cake Cups

For paper products, GB 4806.8 focuses on:

- **Sensory performance:** no detectable odor or taste transfer to food — a direct consumer-protection requirement and a practical quality check for printed or coated papers.
- **Heavy metals:** limits on lead, arsenic, cadmium and related elements migrating from the paper.
- **Fluorescent whitening agents:** controlled because papers containing them are unsuitable for food contact.
- **Formaldehyde and migratable substances:** migration testing under the standard's conditions.

Two scope notes matter to buyers. First, silicone-coated baking parchment is paper with a coating, so both the paper substrate and the silicone layer fall inside the food contact assessment. Second, compliance is tied to the declared use — a report generated for dry, room-temperature contact does not automatically cover hot, fatty contact, which is exactly the use case of baking paper.

## 3. Documentation a Credible Chinese Supplier Should Provide

A manufacturer with real compliance discipline can produce, without friction:

- **A current test report from an accredited laboratory** (CNAS/CMA-accredited domestic labs or international third-party labs), citing the current GB 4806.8 version and covering your product's actual use conditions.
- **A declaration of compliance (DoC)** naming the product, the applicable standards and the responsible legal entity.
- **Production license documentation.** Food-contact paper products fall under China's industrial product licensing regime for food-related products; a live license is a baseline credibility check.
- **Additive and coating statements** mapping to GB 9685, including for silicone coatings and printing inks.
- **Per-lot quality records** such as certificates of analysis — the same discipline that supports export compliance is visible here.

If a supplier can only produce a PDF report without a matching product name, company and date trail, treat that as a finding, not a formality.

## 4. Reading a GB 4806 Test Report Critically

Test reports are the most forged-by-omission documents in the industry. Check:

- **Product match:** does the report describe your product — material, coating, printing — or a "representative sample" chosen by the supplier?
- **Standard version:** outdated GB 4806.8 versions mean the report predates tightened limits.
- **Use conditions:** migration test conditions should reflect hot and fatty contact for baking paper; cold aqueous conditions for chilled dessert cups.
- **Scope gaps:** a report covering the paper but silent on the silicone coating, or on printed inks, is incomplete for your product.
- **Lab identity:** verify the lab is accredited and the report can be validated with the lab directly — most credible labs offer report verification.

The [paper baking cup food contact declarations guide](https://www.lmcakecup.com/resources/paper-baking-cup-food-contact-declarations/) walks through the document set for export markets.

## 5. How GB 4806 Fits with FDA and EU Requirements

GB 4806 is not a substitute for destination-market compliance, and buyers should not treat it as one:

- **For the US**, the paper and its components must satisfy the FDA framework, including 21 CFR 176.170 for paper in contact with aqueous and fatty foods.
- **For the EU**, the material must meet Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 principles and, in practice, national measures and industry standards for paper such as BfR Recommendation XXXVI used across the EU market.
- **Where GB 4806 adds value** is as a mirror: a manufacturer that passes GB 4806.8's migration, heavy metal and sensory requirements with margin, under an accredited lab, is demonstrating the testing infrastructure and process control that export compliance also requires. Use it as a supplier-quality signal, then require the destination-market documents on top.

Buyers sourcing for multiple markets should ask the supplier for a compliance matrix — one row per market, one document per claim.

## 6. A Verification Routine for China-Sourced Paper Packaging

Build the check into onboarding and re-qualification:

1. **License and identity:** verify the production license and legal entity name match the DoC and invoices.
2. **Document trail:** current-version test reports matched to your product and use conditions, from an accredited lab.
3. **Independent confirmation:** for new suppliers or high-volume programs, commission your own draw-down test from retained samples of the actual production lot — retained-sample practice is described in the [retained samples guide](https://www.lmcakecup.com/resources/retained-samples-food-paper-packaging/).
4. **Periodic retesting:** set a retest cycle (annual is common) and make it a contract term.
5. **Audit the system:** an on-site or third-party audit confirms the quality system behind the papers is real — see the [FSSC 22000 supplier guide](https://www.lmcakecup.com/resources/fssc-22000-certified-baking-paper-supplier-guide/) for what certified food-safety systems look like.

## FAQ

### Does GB 4806 apply to baking paper and cake cups?

Yes. GB 4806.8 covers paper and paperboard in contact with food, which includes baking parchment (paper plus silicone coating), cake cups, muffin cups and wrapping papers. Coatings and inks on the product are assessed under the associated additive and general requirements, so request coverage of the whole construction, not just the base paper.

### Is a GB 4806 test report enough to import into the US or EU?

No. GB 4806 is China's domestic standard; import compliance depends on the destination market's rules — the FDA framework for the US and Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 with applicable national measures for the EU. Use GB 4806 evidence to evaluate the manufacturer's quality system, and require destination-market declarations and test reports for import.

### How can I verify a supplier's test report is genuine?

Check the report's lab accreditation (CNAS or CMA for domestic labs), match the product description and dates to your order, confirm the cited standard version is current, and use the lab's report-verification service where available. For important programs, commission an independent test on samples drawn from your own production lot.

### How often should food contact test reports be renewed?

Annually is a common contract term, plus retesting whenever the product construction, coating, ink or raw material changes. Make the retest cycle and change-notification obligations explicit in the supply agreement so compliance does not silently lapse between orders.

## Conclusion

GB 4806 compliance is the clearest window into how a Chinese paper packaging manufacturer actually runs its factory. Read the framework — GB 4806.1, GB 4806.8 and GB 9685 — know what a credible test report looks like, and use that evidence to separate disciplined manufacturers from document-only suppliers. Then layer the FDA and EU paperwork your import markets legally require on top. Suppliers who pass both audits are the ones who hold compliance for years, not shipments.
