## Where Water-Based Coated Base Paper Fits a Paper Bag Programme

Water-based coated base paper is paper coated with a barrier layer applied from a water-based formulation or dispersion. In a bag programme it is the substrate a converter forms, prints, slits and seals into sacks, carrier and food bags. The boundary matters: the coating is on the base paper only, and finished bags, cups, bowls or containers are downstream operations LANGMAI does not supply.

The first specification to fix is the barrier target. Define the contents type, moisture and oil exposure, contact time and temperature instead of assuming one coating fits every use. A dry-goods carrier, a greasy bakery bag and a damp chilled-food sack make different demands on the same coating chemistry, so the end use drives the construction. Environmental wording stays honest the same way: plastic-free, recyclable, repulpable or compostable claims require separate evidence for the exact material.

## 60-120 gsm Selection: Matching the Grade to the Bag

The published reference band for paper-bag base paper is 60 / 70 / 80 / 95 / 120 gsm, sitting inside a wider 60-300 gsm coated range. These are starting points, not stock promises: a ±2 g/m² typical grammage tolerance applies, and the coating side, custom roll width and barrier target by bag use are confirmed before production. Dimensions, coating construction, packaging and acceptance criteria are all settled in the approved specification.

In practice, lighter tiers around 60-80 gsm are candidates for flexible, lightweight bag constructions where the paper wraps around folds and the converting machine needs the web to run flat and tear predictably. Higher tiers toward 95-120 gsm are candidates where handles, stiffness, stackability or heavier carry loads push the construction up. The mapping between tier and bag type is a converting question, not a catalogue lookup. Caliper and stiffness variations within the tolerance window show up on a bag-making line as feeding inconsistency and sealing drift, so the accepted tolerance for the grade should be recorded in the quotation alongside the nominal grammage. The product page's tested reference sample shows how a construction is documented: a 230 g/m² base paper with 8 g/m² coating on each side, nominal 246 g/m² total at a 960 mm nominal width. Not a bag grade and not a limit, but base grammage, coating weight, side and width are each written down separately.

## Coating Side, Sealing and Print on the Converting Line

Once the grammage tier is shortlisted, the construction question becomes directional: is the coating on one side of the web or both, and which face meets the contents? Single-side constructions suit bags where the barrier faces inward and the exterior carries print; double-side constructions are the candidate where both faces need protection or where the sealing system interacts with the coated surface. The answer depends on the forming method, the sealing window and whether exterior print meets the coated face, so coating side is confirmed for the intended application.

Most bag programmes print flexographically, and the ink system is part of the compliance package in food-contact markets. The declaration should name the base paper, the coating system and the ink components together for the destination market.

Slitting and roll width come next. Width is customized to the converter's dimension, so the RFQ should state the exact width and whether edge trim, core size and winding direction follow the bag-making machine's requirements.

## A Converting Test Plan Before You Approve a Grade

A converting test earns its place in the order file only if it runs on the actual equipment under the actual conditions. The plan should cover:

- **Forming and sealing:** run the proposed construction through the actual bag-making line, confirm the sealing window and bond strength with the real contents, and record the settings that pass.
- **Print and converting behaviour:** check flexo print adhesion and definition on the coated face, slitting cleanliness at the production width, and feeding stability across the tolerance band.
- **End-use conditions:** expose sealed samples to the humidity, temperature and contents the bag will actually see, since barrier needs are defined by moisture, oil, contact time and temperature rather than assumed.
- **Documentation fit:** collect the test report references so every claim attached to the grade traces to a documented construction.

The product page's 2025 reference-sample report — a 230 g/m² base with 8 g/m² per side under the listed Chinese standards — supports only its tested items and construction, and must not be generalized to every grammage or finished bags. The [factory and certificate library](/factory-certificates/) lists the third-party audits and test reports behind these claims, and reports must match the quoted construction.

## FAQ

### What grammage of water-based coated paper suits paper bags?

The published reference band for paper-bag base paper is 60 / 70 / 80 / 95 / 120 gsm, with a ±2 g/m² typical grammage tolerance. Lighter tiers are candidates for lightweight flexible constructions and heavier tiers for stiffness and carry-load demands. The mapping is confirmed through converting tests.

### Is water-based coated paper automatically plastic-free or recyclable?

No. "Water-based" describes the coating route, not the dried chemistry or the end-of-life behaviour. Plastic-free, recyclable, repulpable and compostable claims each require separate evidence for the exact construction and the destination market's collection conditions.

### How much sample material does a bag converting trial need?

### What should a paper-bag base paper RFQ include?

Application and contents, target grammage and tolerance, coating side, custom roll width, print requirement, packaging format and destination market. These are the same fields the converting trial records, which keeps the quotation aligned with the approved specification.
