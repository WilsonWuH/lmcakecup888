## Introduction

A certificate PDF arrives with a supplier quotation. It looks official and is dated eleven months ago. Six months later a retail customer asks why a baking paper lot failed incoming inspection, and you discover the certificate covered the group's food plant, not the converting site that made your paper.

FSSC 22000 is one of the strongest signals a food packaging converter can offer. It is recognized by the Global Food Safety Initiative, builds on ISO 22000 with sector prerequisite programs, and adds requirements buyers care about: food fraud mitigation, food defence, allergen control and quality control. But it covers a defined site doing a defined activity, not every carton leaving every building in the group.

This guide covers what the scheme requires of a converter, how it differs from BRCGS Packaging Materials, how to verify a certificate quickly, how to read a scope statement, and what evidence to request before and during an audit.

## 1. What FSSC 22000 Certifies for a Paper Converter

The scheme has three layers. ISO 22000 supplies the management system. A prerequisite program standard matched to the activity sits on top — for baking paper that is Category I, production of packaging material, using ISO/TS 22002-4. Then come additional requirements: control of services and purchased materials, labelling, food defence, food fraud mitigation, allergen management, logo use, environmental monitoring, food safety culture and quality control.

For a converted-paper plant, these should be auditable:

- Incoming control of base paper reels, silicone emulsions, release agents, inks, adhesives and cartons, each with an approved supplier and written specification.
- A hazard analysis specific to converting: foreign bodies, blade control, glass and brittle plastic registers, ink migration, cross-contamination between printed and unprinted runs.
- Process records for silicone coating weight, curing temperature, line speed, slitting, forming and printing.
- Rework control, since off-spec paper re-entering the stream is a frequent finding.
- Traceability with mass-balance testing from finished carton back to paper reel and silicone batch.

Certificate version matters too. Version 6 is current and revised the food chain categories, so a certificate naming an older version was audited against a lighter requirement set.

## 2. How FSSC 22000 Differs From BRCGS Packaging Materials

Both are GFSI-recognized with a packaging route, so either satisfies most mandates. The differences are structural, not a question of which is harder.

BRCGS Packaging Materials is written clause by clause for converters. It is explicit about site and product standards, packaging hazard analysis and grade definitions, and reports graded audit outcomes. European retailers often default to it because the report format is familiar.

FSSC 22000 is a management-system standard applied to packaging. It follows the ISO high-level structure, so it integrates with ISO 9001 and ISO 22000. Findings are reported as nonconformities rather than numeric grades, which usually brings fuller narrative detail.

If your mandate names one scheme, audit to that one. If not, compare suppliers on three things instead of the name: whether the scope covers your exact product family, whether recent findings touch your grades, and whether the certificate stays valid through surveillance audits.

## 3. Verifying the Certificate Is Genuine and Current

Search the Foundation FSSC public register by organization name or by COID, the identification code printed on the certificate. If the site is not listed, ask why. Then check that validity is current rather than suspended or expired; that the certification body is FSSC-licensed with a listed accreditation body; that the certificate references ISO 22000, an appropriate PRP specification and the current scheme version; and that logo, layout and typography match the official format.

Then read the dates. A cycle runs three years with surveillance audits between. A certificate whose surveillance window has passed, or which expires inside your supply period, is a planning problem. Ask for the latest surveillance report and closure evidence for each nonconformity. A supplier unwilling to share its own audit report, redacted if necessary, is telling you something useful.

Note the limits. The FSSC logo belongs in marketing materials, never on labels, cartons or certificates of analysis.

## 4. Scope Coverage: Four Questions That Decide Applicability

- **Which legal entity and site?** Certificates name one organization at one address. If your paper is converted at Plant B while the certificate names Plant A, you have no coverage for your goods.
- **Which category?** For paper packaging you want Category I. A certificate listing only food processing categories suggests experience lies elsewhere.
- **Which processes?** Slitting, sheeting, silicone coating, printing, forming and packing should each be named. A converter that buys pre-coated reels and only slits has a different risk profile from one applying silicone in-house, and usually a different quotation.
- **Which products?** Confirm that rolls, pre-cut sheets, greaseproof sheets and printed baking cups are inside scope. Printing brings ink controls many scopes exclude with wording such as "excluding printed products."

Always request the annex rather than the summary page. The annex is where you see whether your product was actually audited.

## 5. A Buyer Verification Checklist

Before the audit, request the certificate with annex, the latest audit report and nonconformity closures, food-contact declarations for base paper, silicone and inks against your destination regulations, migration testing for fat contact and oven temperature, traceability results, allergen and fraud assessments, pest control and glass registers, coating-weight calibration records and twelve months of complaints.

On site, verify rather than collect. Follow one lot back to its reel identification. Check that blade control and glass registers are current, not updated just before your visit. Confirm physical quarantine of non-conforming stock and a documented rework route. Test one operator's understanding of a control point: records are easy to write, comprehension is hard to fake.

Afterwards, set the rhythm. Refresh documents annually after the surveillance audit, review again after any site move, ownership change or quality incident, and write a change-notification obligation into the supply agreement so relevant changes are reported before shipment.

## Conclusion

Treat an FSSC 22000 certificate as the start of verification, not the end. Confirm it on the register by COID, read the annex for site, category and product coverage, request the latest surveillance report with nonconformity closures, and test traceability on the floor rather than in a meeting room. Suppliers who handle this well are usually the same ones whose coating weights, complaint records and batch documents stay consistent order after order.

### Is FSSC 22000 the same as ISO 22000 certification?

No. ISO 22000 defines the management system; FSSC 22000 adds ISO/TS 22002-4 for packaging plus requirements on food fraud, food defence, allergens and culture. That combination carries GFSI recognition.

### Which food chain category should a baking paper factory hold?

Category I, production of packaging material, using ISO/TS 22002-4. If the certificate lists only food processing categories, ask how the converting activities you buy were assessed.

### Can the FSSC logo be printed on our retail cartons?

No. Logo use is restricted to marketing materials, never labels, food-contact packaging or certificates of analysis, because that implies a product-level approval the certificate does not grant.

### What if the certificate covers a different plant than ours?

You have no certified coverage. Ask whether the producing site holds its own certificate or sits inside a properly audited multi-site structure, and check the address in the annex.

### Does FSSC 22000 certification replace food-contact documents?

No. You still need composition and migration evidence for the base paper, silicone and inks in the exact construction you buy, against your destination market's regulations.
