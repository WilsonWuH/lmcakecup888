When a release agent is misapplied, an ink batch fails migration testing, or a pallet is mixed between grades, the cost of not knowing how a supplier executes a recall lands on the buyer: stock withdrawals, customer complaints and a traceability exercise that fails in front of a regulator or a retail auditor. This guide walks through what triggers a recall in food paper packaging, how lot coding and traceability records should work, what mock recall drills prove, how US and EU notification differs, and what wholesale buyers should expect — and demand — from supplier recall readiness. ## 1. What Triggers a Recall in Food Paper Packaging

Paper is a low-risk material compared with food itself, but it is a food contact material, and recalls happen. The common triggers: Documentation scope is confirmed per SKU and destination market, as outlined on our [pre-cut parchment paper sheets and rounds](/products/baking-parchment-paper/) page.

- Contamination events: foreign matter in the reel (metal fragments from converting equipment, glass, pest evidence), or chemical contamination from machine oils, release agents or unapproved coatings.
- Migration failures: inks, coating components or recycled fiber constituents that fail specific migration limits when tested against the intended food type — fatty bakery goods are the harshest case.
- Wrong material shipped: a non-food-contact grade delivered as food grade, or a documentation mix-up where compliance declarations attached to a shipment belong to a different lot.
- Labeling and declaration errors: incorrect food-contact statements, missing allergen or fragrance claims the buyer relied on, or misdeclared composition.
- Upstream supplier notification: a base paper mill or coating chemistry supplier issues its own alert that propagates down the chain.

The classification matters as much as the event. Serious physical hazard or toxicological risk drives a full recall; a documentation error without health risk may be a market withdrawal or stock correction. Suppliers should define these classifications in writing before anything happens — deciding severity during an incident is how decisions get delayed. ## 2. Traceability Records: Lot Coding as the Backbone

Everything in a recall procedure depends on one capability: taking a customer complaint code and, within hours, producing the full chain in both directions. A workable system for paper packaging looks like this:

- Lot coding at production: each reel and each converting batch carries a unique lot code linking to machine, shift, base paper batch, coating batch and date. Codes are applied on labels and printed on packaging — not held only in a spreadsheet.
- Forward traceability: a log mapping every lot to customer, purchase order, shipment date and quantity, so the supplier can list who received the affected material, down to remaining warehouse stock.
- Backward traceability: a log linking the lot back to raw material inputs — base paper reels, silicone or coating batches, inks — so a defect can be traced to its origin and sibling lots flagged.
- Retention: records kept for at least the shelf life of the packaged food plus a margin; two years is a common contractual minimum, longer where customers or regulators require it.

The practical test is the one auditors use: pick a random finished-goods lot, ask the supplier to trace it forward and backward, and time it. A supplier that needs days, or that cannot connect converting records to base paper batches, has a traceability system only on paper.

## 3. Mock Recall Drills: Testing the System Before It Matters

A recall plan that has never been exercised is a hypothesis. Mock recall drills are how suppliers — and their buyers — find out whether the plan works. A credible drill:

- Is unannounced or randomly selected: an auditor or quality manager picks a lot without warning, not the one prepared in advance.
- Covers both directions: trace the lot to all customers and back to raw material inputs.
- Is timed against a target: a common benchmark is 4 hours to complete both directions, with some customer standards requiring 2 hours for critical suppliers.
- Quantifies recovery: the drill calculates how much affected product is still in the supplier's control versus shipped, and where.
- Ends with a documented review: discrepancies found, root causes, corrective actions, and the date of the next drill — typically once or twice per year.

Buyers should ask for the last mock recall report, not just the procedure. A report showing a 99.8% reconciliation and a 3-hour closure is worth more than a polished 20-page recall policy.

## 4. Regulator Notification: US and EU

Notification obligations differ by market, and suppliers exporting to both need to understand each.

United States: for food contact substances, reportable situations feed the FDA's Reportable Food Registry (RFR). A responsible party must file within 24 hours of determining that a food (including food contact scenarios where substances may migrate) presents a reasonable probability of serious health consequences. Following the report, the supplier works with FDA on remediation and, where a recall occurs, follows FDA recall classification (Class I, II, III) and effectiveness checks.

European Union: Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 requires operators to have traceability systems and to inform competent authorities and customers when a material does not comply or is likely not to comply, so that food it contacts can be withdrawn. Alerts flow through the RASFF (Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed) network operated with EFSA support. There is no fixed 24-hour statutory figure identical to the US RFR, but the practical expectation — and what customer standards demand — is immediate notification upon knowledge, without waiting for complete investigation results.

In both markets the operative principle is the same: notify early with what is known, update as investigation continues. Suppliers who delay notification to "finish the investigation" create liability for their buyers, who may learn of the incident from a retailer or authority first.

## 5. What Wholesale Buyers Should Expect from Supplier Recall Readiness

During qualification or audit, buyers should require evidence, not assurances:

- A written recall and withdrawal procedure with named roles, decision authority and 24/7 contact routes.
- A mock recall report from the last 12 months showing closure time and reconciliation percentage.
- Traceability demonstration: pick a lot during the audit and ask for both-direction tracing on the spot.
- Defined notification timelines and templates for customer alerts, including the data fields buyers need (lot codes, quantities, ship dates, affected SKUs).
- Insurance and crisis-management arrangements proportionate to the volumes supplied.
- Contractual alignment: recall cost allocation, cooperation clauses and record retention stated in the supply agreement.

A supplier that answers all six fluently is rare; that is exactly why it is a differentiator worth scoring in supplier selection, not an optional extra.

## Conclusion

Recall readiness in food paper packaging comes down to three verifiable things: lot-level traceability that closes in hours, mock recall drills with documented results, and notification procedures aligned to FDA and EU requirements. Buyers who audit these three — and put timelines and cost allocation into contracts — turn a supplier's crisis from their own crisis into a managed event with a partner who knows exactly where every pallet went. Our [custom printed baking parchment paper](/products/baking-parchment-paper/) programme is audited against the same standards referenced in this guide.

### How quickly must a food packaging supplier notify customers of a potential recall?

Best practice — and most customer standards — require notification within 24 hours of a confirmed or suspected food-safety issue, with preliminary data rather than waiting for a full investigation.

### What is a mock recall drill?

A simulated exercise where a random lot is traced forward to all customers and backward to raw materials against a time target, producing a documented report of reconciliation percentage and corrective actions.

### How long should traceability records for food packaging be kept?

A minimum of two years after shipment is a common contractual baseline; longer periods apply where customer specifications or local regulation require it.

### Is recall notification mandatory for paper packaging in the EU?

Yes. Under Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004, operators must inform competent authorities and customers when food contact material is non-compliant, enabling withdrawal of affected food.
