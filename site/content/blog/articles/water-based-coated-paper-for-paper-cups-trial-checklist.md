## Cup-Body Grammage: Mapping the 60–300 gsm Range to the Forming Line

Grammage is the first filter because it is tied to the container being formed. Cup, bowl and food-container stock on the water-based coated range is referenced in two tiers: 170–210 gsm for lighter cup bodies, and 230 / 250 / 280 / 300 gsm for heavier constructions where wall stiffness and denesting behaviour matter as much as barrier performance. Tolerance is the detail converters most often overlook: the typical ±2 g/m² spread matters on a forming line because caliper and stiffness variations show up as feeding inconsistencies and flange-sealing drift, so the accepted tolerance for the grade should be recorded in the approved specification and written quotation. A useful anchor is the tested reference sample on the product page: a 230 g/m² base paper with 8 g/m² coating on each side, nominal 246 g/m² total at a 960 mm nominal width — not a limit, since roll width is customized to the converter's dimension, but a demonstration of how a construction is written down: base grammage, coating weight, side and width.

## Coating Side: Single-Side, Double-Side and Where the Barrier Has to Sit

The second specification item is coating side, confirmed per application rather than assumed. On a cup-forming line the question is which surface faces the filled product and which surface the machine seals: a food-contact coating on the inside face, a machine-side surface tuned for forming and flange bonding, or a double-side construction where both faces are coated — as in the tested 230 + 8 + 8 g/m² reference sample. Coating side interacts with the sealing window, because bonding behaviour at the flange depends on which surfaces meet there, and with print adhesion on the coated outside face. The practical rule is to declare the construction as the forming line sees it — food-contact side, machine side, print timing, sealing method — so the mill can confirm whether a single-side or double-side construction fits and the trial runs as a controlled test. ## A Forming and Sealing Trial Checklist for Cup-Body Stock

The trial is where a cup programme is decided, and it is worth running against a written checklist:

- **Grammage and tolerance on record.** Confirm target grammage, the ±2 g/m² typical tolerance and the accepted tolerance for the exact grade before the trial roll is slit.
- **Coating side declared.** Mark the food-contact face and the machine face on the sample roll so every observation maps to the right surface.
- **Width as slit.** Run the trial at the production width, since edge behaviour and feeding change with width.
- **Forming and sealing window mapped.** Record the sealing parameters that produce a sound flange bond on the coated construction, not just the line defaults.
- **Fill-condition leak testing.** Test with the actual fill temperatures and dwell times the cup will see, and log leakage against the sealing window.
- **Print behaviour.** Where cups are printed, judge ink adhesion and registration on the coated outside face.
- **Sensory check.** Screen the formed cup for odor or taint against the filled product.
- **Documentation pack.** Collect trial parameters, roll construction and test documents so the quote references the approved specification.

## FAQ

### Which grammage should a converter start with for paper cup bodies?

Start from the container, not the coating. Lighter cup-body constructions are referenced at 170–210 gsm, heavier cup and bowl constructions at 230 / 250 / 280 / 300 gsm where wall stiffness and denesting matter. The machine, fill conditions and flange design narrow the choice; the trial confirms it.

### Does coating side really change the trial result?

Yes. The flange bond forms between specific surfaces, so which face is coated determines how the sealing window behaves, how print adheres outside, and which surface faces the filled product. Single-side and double-side constructions of the same grammage can behave differently on the same line.

### Can a converter request a sample on its own specification?

Yes. A 200 m sample length can be provided in any requested specification — grammage, coating side, width and shipping arrangements are confirmed before dispatch. Sampling on the intended construction makes the trial predictive, because results transfer to production only when the construction matches.

### Does the GB test report prove the paper is recyclable or compostable?

No. The reviewed report supports its tested sample and the listed food-contact items only. Recyclability, repulpability and compostability require product-specific evidence for the exact construction and destination market.
