## Introduction

A claim printed on a carton of baking cups invites the assumption that the paper inside came from a responsibly managed forest. Often that is exactly what it means. Sometimes it says something narrower, and the difference surfaces at the worst moment, during a retailer sustainability audit, when the buyer cannot produce evidence.

The gap is rarely fraud. It is almost always structural. A pleated baking cup sits at the end of a chain that may include a pulp mill, a paper mill, a silicone coater, a printer and a converter, and a claim survives only if every organisation taking legal ownership holds valid certification and passes it correctly at each transfer. One uncertified link, or one invoice missing the claim language, and the claim stops there.

Managing this is administrative discipline rather than technical work. This guide covers what certification covers, how it moves through conversion, what the three labels mean, how to verify a certificate and invoice, and which documents belong in an audit-ready file.

## 1. What FSC Certification Actually Certifies

FSC operates two distinct certification types, and confusing them causes most downstream problems.

Forest Management certification applies to forests. It confirms that a defined forest unit is managed against FSC requirements covering environmental values, workers' rights, community relations and monitoring. No factory ever holds this.

Chain of Custody certification applies to organisations taking legal ownership of certified material: traders, mills, coaters, printers, converters and distributors. It requires inputs and outputs to be tracked, certified and non-certified material to be controlled, and any claim on a sales document to reflect what is sold.

Only chain of custody matters directly to a baking paper buyer, because it is what allows a supplier to sell with a claim attached. A mill presenting photographs of certified forests is answering a question nobody asked, while a converter without its own valid certificate number cannot make the claim at all.

## 2. How the Claim Moves Through Conversion

Trace the chain before signing anything. A typical route runs from pulp mill to paper mill, onward to a silicone coater, sometimes to a printer, then to the converter that pleats and forms cups.

Two rules govern the path. Every organisation taking legal ownership must hold valid chain of custody certification, including traders who never touch the goods. And a claim transfers on the sales document, so each invoice must carry the seller's certificate code and the correct claim.

Servicing steps behave differently. A converter processing material owned by someone else under a toll arrangement must still be covered, under its own certificate or the certificate holder's controlled subcontractor arrangements. Ask which applies and get it in writing.

For the buyer the requirement is narrower than most expect. Buying certified goods without restating the claim requires evidence of valid transfer: a compliant invoice and a valid supplier certificate. Placing a claim on your own packaging or marketing requires your own certificate and trademark approval, a step frequently overlooked.

## 3. What the Three Label Types Mean

FSC offers three on-product claims, and they are not equivalent.

FSC 100% means all fibre comes from FSC-certified forests, with a limited allowance for fibre that cannot practically be segregated. It is the strongest claim and the most constrained on supply and price.

FSC Recycled means the product is made from reclaimed post-consumer or pre-consumer material. This label carries no claim about forest management, and buyers selling for direct food contact should confirm the recycled input meets food-contact requirements, since recycled fibre changes the contamination risk profile.

FSC Mix combines certified forest material, recycled material and controlled wood. Controlled wood is not certified forest; it is material screened to exclude unacceptable sources such as illegally harvested timber or conversion of natural forest. A credit sale is backed by equivalent certified input somewhere in the production system rather than by certified fibre physically present in that batch. Confirm in writing which claim is intended.

## 4. Verifying the Certificate and the Invoice

Verification takes minutes and prevents most problems. Do it before first delivery and repeat at every renewal.

Check the certificate in FSC's public database using the supplier's code, an FSC-C prefix followed by digits. Confirm the certificate is current and not suspended or withdrawn; the legal entity matches the seller on your invoice; the scope covers chain of custody; and product categories include paper or converted paper products. Note the expiry date, since a lapsed certificate invalidates every subsequent claim.

Then check the invoice. A compliant document states the seller's certificate code, identifies each product line sold with a claim, uses the correct wording and records quantities. Phrases such as sustainably sourced carry no traceability. Keep those documents: a declaration letter without matching dated invoices will not satisfy an auditor.

## 5. Why Retail ESG Programmes Push It Down the Chain

Large retailers and food brands set packaging sourcing policies covering deforestation risk, responsible fibre and traceability, then extend them across suppliers. Certification appeals to them because it converts a difficult question into a checkable one: a certificate number that can be looked up, a claim on an invoice, and a third-party audit behind both.

That cascades quickly. A bakery supplying a supermarket may be asked to show certified paper cups, which requires the converter to supply the claim, which requires the mills behind it. Once written into a supplier code, it becomes a standing line in every subsequent questionnaire.

There is also a defensive reason to move ahead of demand. Buyers who establish documented supply before a mandate lands avoid emergency re-qualification under a deadline, which is when specification compromises happen. Where certification across the whole range is impractical, qualify the highest-visibility lines first.

## 6. Documents to Hold for Audit Readiness

Keep one file per supplier and update it annually:

- The supplier's valid chain of custody certificate, showing scope, product categories and expiry date.
- Confirmation of your own database check, with date.
- Dated invoices showing certificate code, product identification and claim wording.
- Written confirmation of which claim is supplied, and whether under percentage or credit.
- Any subcontracted coating or printing step, and how it is covered.
- If you print claims on your own packaging, your certificate and trademark approval.
- Change-notification terms requiring advice on suspension, withdrawal, lapse or scope reduction.
- Records reconciling certified purchases against certified sales, where you restate a claim.

## Conclusion

Buying certified baking paper is a documentation exercise wrapped around a physical product. The material performs the same whether or not the paperwork is right, which is why the paperwork gets neglected. The discipline is cheap: trace ownership including traders and toll converters, verify the certificate in the public database before first delivery and at each renewal, insist every invoice carries certificate code and correct claim wording, and hold those documents ready for inspection. Decide deliberately which of the three claims you need and write it into the specification.

### What is the difference between FSC 100%, FSC Mix and FSC Recycled?

FSC 100% means all fibre comes from FSC-certified forests. Mix combines certified forest material, recycled material and controlled wood, which is screened but not certified. Recycled means reclaimed material and carries no claim about forest management. Confirm in writing which one is supplied.

### Do I need my own FSC certificate to buy certified baking cups?

Usually not to buy them. Purchasing certified goods requires evidence the claim was validly transferred to you, meaning a valid supplier certificate and a compliant invoice. Putting a claim on your own packaging or marketing requires your own chain of custody certificate and the associated trademark approval.

### How do I verify a supplier's certificate?

Use the supplier's certificate code, in the form FSC-C followed by digits, in FSC's public certificate database. Confirm the certificate is current and not suspended, the legal entity matches your seller, the scope covers chain of custody, the product categories include paper products, and note the expiry date for renewal reminders.

### What should appear on the invoice for the claim to be valid?

The seller's certificate code, identification of each product sold with a claim, the claim itself using correct wording such as FSC Mix Credit, and the quantity. Phrases like sustainably sourced without certificate code and claim wording carry no traceability and will not satisfy an auditor.

### Can a toll converter without its own certificate handle certified material?

It must still be covered, under its own certification or the certificate holder's controlled subcontractor arrangements. Ask which mechanism applies and get it in writing, because an undocumented processing step breaks the claim.
